Pixel Lily

A pink stargazer lily in chunky pixel art, grown with two hands. My left hand pinches the flower open, and snapping the pinch shut plucks the petals. My right hand looks after the stem: an open palm grows it and adds leaves, a fist wilts it. Hold the fist until everything dies, and the next open palm grows a different kind of flower, six kinds in turn. The stem hand is read by a convolutional neural network that I train in the browser on pictures of my own hand.

The idea

The camera picture is split down the middle by a pink line. The left side belongs to the flower hand and the right side to the stem hand.

Why two different models? The class example is a classifier. It answers “which of my labels is this picture?”, so it fits the stem hand well: open, fist, or no hand at all. A pinch is a distance, though, not a category. A classifier can't tell me how open my fingers are, so the flower hand uses handPose keypoints (thumb tip to index tip, divided by the palm length so it reads the same near or far from the camera). Closing slowly closes the flower. Only a snap, from at least 0.55 open to almost touching within 0.4 seconds, plucks the petals.

Until the CNN is trained, handPose stands in for the stem hand too, so the lily responds right away. It compares how far the four fingertips reach from the wrist with how far their knuckles are (about 1.8–2 with the palm open, about 1 in a fist). The sketch shows yellow dots on those fingertips. Once you train, the CNN takes over.

Six kinds of flower. Each time a fist kills the garden, the next open palm starts it again as a different flower. The lily has its own drawing. The other five are rings of petals that start bunched into a green bud and spread out as they open, all the way round or (for the tulip) into a cup, some around a speckled center disc. The rose stacks three rings. The new kind's name shows above the garden for a few seconds.

What the CNN sees

camera (mirrored) → right half, 320×480 → squished to 64×64 → pixels −0.5…0.5 → CNN → open / fist / none → the stem

This builds on the class example Training a Webcam Image Classifier with Convolutional Layers. The example grabbed the whole canvas with get(). My canvas is the garden, so I grab straight from the camera with video.get(), and only its right half. p5's flipped: true mirrors the actual pixels, not just the preview (I checked the p5 1.11.10 source), so the right half in code is the right half you see. The sketch draws the 64×64 picture next to the camera preview so you can watch what the network is really looking at.

ml5 builds this network for task: "imageClassification". These are the shapes from its model summary:

LayerOutputWeightsWhat it does
input64×64×4–the crop: red, green, blue, alpha per pixel
conv2d60×60×88088 filters, each 5×5 pixels, slid across the picture. Each one makes a feature map that lights up wherever its pattern (an edge, a blob of skin) appears. ReLU then sets the negatives to 0.
max pooling30×30×80keeps the strongest value in every 2×2 block. Half the size, and a finger can shift a pixel without changing the result.
conv2d26×26×163,21616 filters that look at the first layer's maps, so they find patterns made of patterns (fingers spread vs. curled)
max pooling13×13×160halves it again
flatten + dense38,115every remaining value votes for open, fist or none. Softmax turns the votes into confidences that add up to 1.

That's 12,139 weights, all learned from about a hundred pictures. The plant only reacts when the CNN is at least 60% sure. Anything less counts as “none”, so a confused guess doesn't wilt the lily.

Recording examples works like my Assignment 7: press and hold a label (or hold 1, 2 or 3) and pictures stream in about 9 times a second while you move your hand around. The variety matters, because the network learns everything in the crop, including the background and my shirt.

Why it only ever said “a”

Before building any of this, I ran the class example unchanged: open palm as A, fist as B, train. It only ever guessed a. Two things were probably going on.

1. The hand was a tiny part of the picture. The example looks at the whole frame, so my face and the room took up most of every example, and open and fist looked almost the same. Cropping to the stem-hand half made the hand a much bigger share of what the network sees.

2. Training got stuck. I tested ml5's CNN on generated pictures (a big blob for open, a small blob for fist, nothing for none, plus noise) to find settings that train reliably, 3 labels, 20 pictures each:

SettingResult
ml5 default (SGD, learning rate 0.02), easy pictureslearned: loss 0.004, 24/30 new pictures right
learning rate 0.1 (what the class digit example uses)stuck at 1.09 = coin-flip for 3 labels; on the fake camera it died at 5.8
ml5 default, low-contrast “webcam-like” picturesflaky: one run stuck at coin-flip, one slow, one spiked to 4.5 at the end
batch normalization layers addeddead every time, loss frozen at 5.799
pixels centered on 0 (−0.5…0.5 instead of 0…1)learned in 3/3 runs
centered + batches of 8 instead of 4 (what the sketch uses)learned in 5/5 runs, 17–26 of 30 new pictures right

Centering is a tiny change (/ 255 - 0.5) that matters because of how SGD moves the weights. With every input between 0 and 1, every input is positive, so all the weights in a filter get pushed the same way on each step. Centered inputs can push in both directions. These were generated pictures, not my hand, so the real test is the loss line in the sketch: with 3 labels it should end well under 1.1, and the sketch warns when it doesn't.

Testing

Does the CNN learn through the real camera path? Chrome can pretend a video file is the webcam. I generated a 6-second loop where the stem-hand side shows a big bright disc (labeled open), a small disc (fist) or nothing (none), with a marker in the corner saying which. The sketch collected 40 crops of each from the live camera path (mirror, right half, 64×64, centered), trained, then its live guesses were compared to the marker:

Trained on 120 pictures · loss 0.81 → 0.000
live guesses matching the clip: 29/30
mean crop brightness: open 0.033 · fist −0.042 · none −0.058

The brightness line proves the crop is the correct half: the discs only exist on the stem-hand side, and the crops get brighter with disc size. If I had cropped the wrong half, all three would read the same.

The plant, with real mouse and key events. A script drove headless Chrome through every state (mouse mode: drag across the garden to pinch, hold O / F for the stem). Holding O for 4 s grew the stem 22 → 80 px and added leaves. Dragging right bloomed it to 0.95. Closing slowly to 0.4 plucked nothing. A snap plucked once, and the 6 petals fell and landed. Holding F for 2.2 s wilted it fully, and O brought it back. Log: mouse-run-2026-10-28.txt.

The rules. 20 node tests on lily-logic.js: the pinch reads the same anywhere in the frame and at any distance, broken (NaN) hands are ignored, slow closing never plucks, snapping plucks exactly once and regrows, a snap from a closed flower does nothing, open grows up to a limit, a fist wilts without shrinking, no hands means nothing changes, the handPose stand-in tells open from fist, the sun rises and fades, the little lilies and the meadow sprout in order and only with an open palm, fallen petals dissolve on time, a fist only switches the flower once everything is dead (and only once per death), dying is announced exactly once, and the six kinds come round in order.

Not tested yet: a live run with my own two hands. Everything above uses a fake camera, the mouse, or generated pictures.

Try it

  1. Open the sketch and click Start camera.
  2. Right hand on the right side of the pink line. Hold open and wave your open palm around, then the same for fist, and none with no hand there (pinching with your left hand is fine). About 30 of each.
  3. Train, and watch the loss line drop below the faint coin-flip line.
  4. Grow it with your palm, pinch it open with your left hand, snap to pluck, fist to wilt.

No camera? Use mouse + keys: drag across the garden to pinch (fast to the left to pluck), hold O to grow, F to wilt. Save model downloads the trained CNN, and Load model… brings it back without retraining.

Limits & next

  • The CNN learns my room, my lighting and my sleeves along with my hand. Someone else probably has to train their own, which is why training happens right on the page.
  • If I save a model trained on my hand into sketch/model/, the sketch loads it by itself, so a demo can skip training.
  • The handPose stand-in already tells open from fist from keypoints, with rules I wrote. Comparing it against the trained CNN on the same hand would show what learning from raw pixels gets right and wrong.
  • Next: choose the flower with a hand shape (a new CNN label per kind) instead of cycling through them, and send bloom and wilt to TouchDesigner like my Assignment 6.
